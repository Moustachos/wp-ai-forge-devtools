# Shiftloom: staff scheduling for teams that work in shifts

Shiftloom is scheduling software for businesses whose people don't all work office hours: cafés and restaurants, retail stores, clinics, warehouses, community centers, anywhere the rota changes every week and someone has to make it fit. It replaces the spreadsheet, the group chat and the sticky note on the break-room door with one place where availability, shifts and swaps live together.

We built it because building a schedule by hand is slow, and because the hard part isn't the grid. It's everything around it: chasing people for their availability, remembering who can't close on Fridays, noticing that a swap left the morning without a keyholder, and telling everyone when something changes.

## What Shiftloom does

At its core, Shiftloom does a small number of things and tries to do them well.

It collects availability and time-off requests from your staff, directly from their phones. It lets a manager draft a schedule on a weekly grid, with the rules of the business built in: who is trained for which station, who needs a break after a long shift, which roles must always be covered. It publishes the finished rota to everyone at the same time and keeps it up to date when shifts change hands. And it keeps a record of what was planned and what actually happened, so payroll and the next schedule start from facts rather than memory.

There is no separate app for managers and staff. Everyone uses the same product and sees what their role allows them to see.

## Who it's for

Shiftloom is designed for small and mid-sized teams run by people who have other jobs besides scheduling. The typical person building a rota in Shiftloom is a store manager, a head chef, a practice coordinator or an owner who also works the floor.

It works best when the team is large enough that the schedule no longer fits in someone's head, but small enough that the person writing it still knows everyone by name. We haven't built it for enterprise workforce planning, union rule engines or multi-country payroll, and we'd rather say so up front than let you find out halfway through a trial.

If your business runs several locations, each one gets its own schedule and its own manager view, and staff who work across locations see all their shifts in one list.

## How a schedule gets built

Every rota in Shiftloom goes through the same sequence of steps. You can move quickly through them when you know the tool, but the order stays the same, because each step depends on the one before it.

**Setting the frame.** Before anyone is asked for anything, the manager sets up the week's frame. That means the opening hours for each day, the stations or roles that need to be covered, and the minimum staffing for each part of the day. A café might need a barista and a cashier from opening, another barista through the lunch rush and a closer who holds the keys. These are templates: you set them up at the start and adjust them when the business changes, for a holiday week, a stocktake or a private event.

**Collecting availability.** When the frame exists, Shiftloom asks staff for their availability for the coming period. Each person gets a notification on their phone and fills in when they can and can't work, marks any time off they want to request, and adds a note if something is uncertain. Recurring constraints, like a student who has classes on the same afternoons every semester, are saved so they don't have to be entered again. The manager sees responses arrive in real time and can nudge anyone who hasn't answered with a single tap instead of a round of messages.

**Reviewing time off.** Time-off requests land in their own queue. The manager approves or declines them before drafting, so the draft is built on settled ground. When colleagues ask for the same days off, Shiftloom shows it side by side with who else is available for those days, which makes the decision easier to explain.

**Drafting the grid.** Now the manager opens the draft. Shiftloom can propose an initial version that fills the frame from the available people, respecting their availability, their training and the rules you set: no clopening shifts back to back, a break after a long day, a cap on weekly hours for anyone who asked for one. The proposal is a starting point, never a decision. The manager moves shifts around by dragging them, swaps people, splits a long shift or leaves a gap open on purpose. Every change is checked as it happens, and anything that breaks a rule is flagged in the grid rather than refused, because sometimes the manager knows something the software doesn't.

**Checking coverage and cost.** Alongside the grid, Shiftloom shows where coverage is thin, where it's heavier than it needs to be, and how the planned hours compare with the budget you set for the week. Nothing here is automatic. The point is to make the trade-offs visible before the rota goes out, not to optimize them away.

**Filling open shifts.** Shifts the manager leaves open can be offered to the team. Eligible staff see them on their phones and can pick one up; the manager decides whether to confirm it automatically or review each claim.

**Publishing.** When the draft looks right, the manager publishes it. Everyone on the rota gets a notification with their own shifts, and the full schedule becomes visible to the team according to your settings. Published schedules can still change, but from this point every change is tracked and every affected person is notified. There's no more wondering whether someone saw the updated version pinned in the break room.

**After publishing.** Real weeks don't go to plan. When someone needs to give up a shift, they offer it to colleagues who are trained for the same role, and the swap goes to the manager for approval if you want that step. When someone calls in sick, the manager can post the shift as open or reassign it directly. Clock-ins, if you use them, are compared with the published rota at the end of the week, and the differences are ready for payroll without anyone reconciling paper timesheets.

That record becomes the input to the next cycle. The following week's draft starts from what actually happened, which is how the schedule gets a little easier to build each time.

## For the people on the rota

A schedule only works if the people on it can read it. Staff use Shiftloom from their phones, in a browser or in the mobile app, and the home screen they see is simply their next shifts: where, when, which role, and who else is working.

From there they can update their availability, request time off, offer a shift to colleagues or pick up an open one. They don't need to learn a manager's tool, and they never see anything they don't need, such as other people's time-off reasons or the week's labor budget.

Notifications are kept deliberately quiet. Staff are told when their own shifts are published or changed, when a swap they asked for is approved, and when an open shift they're eligible for appears, if they've chosen to hear about those. They aren't pinged every time the manager moves someone else's shift around in a draft.

## Payroll and the tools you already use

Shiftloom doesn't try to be your payroll system. At the end of each pay period, it produces a clean record of the hours worked by each person, with the differences from the published rota already highlighted, and hands it to the payroll tool you already use, either through a direct connection or as a file your accountant can import.

The same goes for the rest of your setup. Published shifts can appear in each person's own calendar, and point-of-sale or time clock systems can send clock-ins back to Shiftloom so the actual hours don't have to be typed in by hand. If a connection you need doesn't exist yet, tell us: the list of integrations we build next comes mostly from those requests.

## Your data stays yours

Schedules, availability and hours belong to the business that entered them. You can export your data at any point in plain formats that open in any spreadsheet, and if you leave Shiftloom, you take everything with you, including the history of past schedules.

We store only what scheduling needs: names, contact details for notifications, roles, availability and hours. We don't sell data, we don't use it for advertising, and staff personal notes about availability are visible only to the managers who schedule them. When a staff member leaves your team, you decide whether their history is kept for payroll records or removed.

## Setting up your team

Getting a team into Shiftloom is mostly a matter of describing how your business already works. You add your locations, the roles people fill and the opening hours, then invite staff by email or text message. Each person sets up their own account and fills in their availability, so the manager doesn't have to collect it by hand at the outset either.

If you're coming from a spreadsheet, you can import the list of staff and their roles instead of typing them in. Past schedules don't need to be imported to get started; the history builds itself as soon as a rota is published.

Most of the setup can be changed later without starting over. Roles can be renamed, rules adjusted and templates edited as you learn what your team actually needs, and none of those changes touch schedules that are already published.
